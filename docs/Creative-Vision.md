# Creative Vision

[Project index](../README.md) · [Current status and open questions](Project-Status.md)

Status: **Confirmed project direction**, approved in the user's initialization brief on October 31, 2026. Implementation preferences are identified separately below; they are not final technical decisions. No setting lore, protagonist, profession, or story has been established.

## Purpose and anthology form

Develop a coherent, richly developed original world experienced through interconnected short-to-medium-length playable stories. The central goal is a world worth understanding, explored through a character who can investigate, negotiate, form relationships, and fight. Players should live through events, shape their approach, and encounter responses to what they actually do.

Each story should offer a distinctive local setting, memorable people, a conflict rooted in the world, meaningful player involvement, and consequences appropriate to the player's actions. Shared characters, places, institutions, themes, and consequences can gradually reveal the wider setting. The anthology should support variety and depth within manageable solo-development scope.

Narrative references are *The Last Wish* and *Sword of Destiny* for interconnected stories, folklore, characterization, social commentary, and morally complicated human conflicts; and *The Witcher 3* for investigations, relationships, memorable side quests, and consequences. Study and transform their narrative principles without reproducing their distinctive fictional elements.

## People, conflict, and tone

- Prioritize believable motivations, relationships, and understandable but sometimes incompatible desires.
- Let social structures, institutions, traditions, and prejudices materially influence events.
- Derive moral complexity from circumstances. Avoid artificial binary choices and contrived ambiguity.
- Let discovering the truth complicate decisions, with consequences beyond conventional success/failure or good/evil outcomes.
- Allow the world to develop independently of the protagonist.
- Include compassion, humor, courage, cruelty, love, absurdity, and tragedy. Darkness should allow emotional variety.
- Favor intimate, character-driven conflicts over obligatory world-ending stakes.
- Avoid excessive nihilism, arbitrary twists, universal corruption, and repetitive dilemmas.
- Not every story requires supernatural danger, conspiracy, violence, or tragedy.
- Make incomplete information believable rather than deliberately manipulative.

## Worldbuilding approach

Develop substantial world foundations before selecting the protagonist's profession or central narrative role. Discover the people, institutions, needs, and conflicts that arise from the setting.

Explore identity and tone; the supernatural in ordinary life; geography, environment, resources, and material conditions; history and its consequences; cultures, governments, religions, economies, and hierarchies; everyday customs and relationships; institutions and professions; and tensions that can generate stories. These are connected areas of inquiry, not a mandatory sequence.

Favor causal coherence: institutions, customs, and historical developments need understandable origins and consequences. Give cultures meaningful internal diversity. Examine how ordinary people experience political, economic, and supernatural conditions.

Use hypothetical situations and short stories to test ideas. Connect subjects rather than collecting isolated lore. Build enough depth to support many narratives beyond the first playable story, while avoiding encyclopedic detail without creative value.

## Protagonist and profession

The favored direction is a middle ground between a predefined character and a blank-slate avatar: an established profession, training, background, and circumstances may coexist with player-shaped personality, values, relationships, specialization, and decisions. This is a creative preference, not an approved identity or biography.

The witcher profession is a narrative reference because it connects travel, specialized expertise, investigations, danger, social access, distrust, and limited political power. Apparent assignments can expose underlying human conflicts. Seek an original profession with comparable narrative usefulness, emerging from this world's history and needs rather than renaming or superficially altering a witcher.

## Gameplay priorities

The five pillars are **exploration, investigation, social interaction and negotiation, relationships, and combat**. Each deserves meaningful attention and mechanical support; these are priorities rather than finalized systems.

Players should be able to favor approaches without every approach being equally effective in every situation. Systems should interact: exploration can uncover evidence; investigation can change negotiations; relationships can bring help or complications; violence can end an immediate threat while producing lasting consequences.

Favor authored situations that respond believably to actions and inaction. Avoid oppressive or arbitrary hidden timers, disconnected minigames, rigid solution paths, and reducing every conflict to dialogue checks or combat.

## Development preferences and feasibility

The intended game is primarily a solo project with limited development time. Current preferences are PC first; strong controller support, especially Steam Deck; Godot as the likely engine; grounded, atmospheric, stylized visuals, potentially retro-inspired low-poly 3D; reusable mechanics, environments, and assets; and compact but meaningful playable spaces.

Assess implementation ideas through content production, development time, testing, maintenance, and reuse. Seek smaller scope that enables deeper stories, without prematurely restricting creative exploration.

Worldbuilding and narrative development are the immediate priority. Code, game assets, and technical architecture require explicit instruction. Unresolved design and production choices are maintained in [Project Status](Project-Status.md#open-questions).
